Garima Chaubey

Ph.D. candidate, researching how AI systems learn, adapt, and remember across a lifetime overcoming #Catastrophic-Forgetting.

I am a Ph.D. researcher at the Indian Institute of Information Technology, Allahabad, where my work sits at the intersection of continual learning, representation learning, and multi-agent reinforcement environments.

My research explores how artificial agents can retain past knowledge while flexibly adapting to new tasks — a question motivated by both cognitive science and the practical demands of lifelong AI. I design hybrid frameworks combining Reinforced Continual Learning, Bayesian optimization, and CLIP-based representation learning to balance stability and plasticity in evolving environments.

Before my doctoral studies, I completed an M.Sc. in Computational Science and Applications from Banaras Hindu University, where my thesis developed a Bayesian Logistic Regression framework for modelling well-being outcomes among university staff.

Beyond research, I enjoy reading about statistical theory, exploring ideas in neuroscience, and mentoring juniors interested in applied machine learning.

Continual Learning in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Environments

Ongoing · Ph.D. Thesis
  • Exploring continual learning strategies for multi-agent settings, focusing on knowledge retention and efficient adaptation using LoRA to minimize catastrophic forgetting.
  • Investigating CLIP-based representation learning to leverage shared vision–language embeddings for improved task generalization and alignment across sequential tasks.
  • Designing hybrid frameworks combining Reinforced Continual Learning (RCL) and Bayesian optimization to balance stability and plasticity in evolving environments.
  • Benchmarking baseline methods across Regularization, Replay, and Architecture-based approaches to analyze scalability and interference behavior in lifelong learning systems.

A Study of Fairness and Bias in Deep Learning Models

Published · CVMI 2025
  • Investigated systemic bias across deep learning architectures — LeNet-5, ResNet, and Vision Transformers (ViT) — using diverse statistical fairness matrices.
  • Analyzed the emergence of bias throughout the AI lifecycle, from data acquisition and model design to real-world deployment practices.
  • Implemented model retraining and adjustment techniques to observe and quantify improvements in fairness outcomes and ethical performance.
  • Developed a structured evaluation framework to support the design of equitable AI technologies, providing a comprehensive view of bias estimation and mitigation in deep learning systems.
#Fairness #Vision-Transformers #ResNet #Ethical-AI

Statistical Analysis of Job Satisfaction, Stress & Happiness Among BHU Staff

M.Sc. Thesis · BHU
  • Developed an integrated Bayesian Logistic Regression framework to quantify determinants of stress and happiness among 500+ university staff, overcoming small-sample biases via Bootstrap Resampling.
  • Implemented non-informative Cauchy Priors (location 0, scale 2.5) to ensure model robustness, achieving stable convergence for high-dimensional institutional data where classical MLE estimates were unstable.
  • Executed Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulations with 4 independent chains and 5,000+ iterations per chain, validating results through trace plot mixing.
  • Identified factors affecting job satisfaction, stress and happiness for staff wellbeing at BHU.
  • Presented actionable insights to improve institutional productivity by isolating significant Intercept values (-14.65), highlighting the influence of baseline institutional culture on employee mental health.
#Bayesian-Inference #MCMC #Bootstrap #Logistic-Regression
