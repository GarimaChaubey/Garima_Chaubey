Statistical Analysis of Job Satisfaction, Stress & Happiness Among BHU Staff

M.Sc. Thesis · BHU
  • Developed an integrated Bayesian Logistic Regression framework to quantify determinants of stress and happiness among 500+ university staff, overcoming small-sample biases via Bootstrap Resampling.
  • Implemented non-informative Cauchy Priors (location 0, scale 2.5) to ensure model robustness, achieving stable convergence for high-dimensional institutional data where classical MLE estimates were unstable.
  • Executed Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulations with 4 independent chains and 5,000+ iterations per chain, validating results through trace plot mixing.
  • Identified factors affecting job satisfaction, stress and happiness for staff wellbeing at BHU.
  • Presented actionable insights to improve institutional productivity by isolating significant Intercept values (-14.65), highlighting the influence of baseline institutional culture on employee mental health.
